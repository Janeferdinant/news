Status: **Verified (re-run 2026-10-13 on regenerated synthetic data, seed=42)**.

> **These numbers describe synthetic, template-generated articles. They say nothing about real-world fake-news detection.** The "fake" and "real" classes differ only by a writing-style recipe built into `data/generate_synthetic.py`, and that recipe deliberately overlaps (about 15% of articles of each label are written in the other label's style), which keeps accuracy well below 100% (the ceiling was not measured). ## Why the data was regenerated

The original `fake_news_dataset.csv` is not in the repository and the owner does not remember where it came from. `data/generate_synthetic.py` was added on 2026-10-13 to produce a replacement with the same seven columns the notebook expects (`title, text, date, source, author, category, label`). It does not recreate the original rows.

## Protocol

- Split first: stratified 80/20 split of the 20,000 articles with `random_state=42`: 16,000 train and 4,000 test rows (2,000 per class in the test set).
- TF-IDF (5,000 uni- and bigram features) is fitted on the training articles only and applied to the test articles. Three engineered features (text length, title length, sentiment) are appended: 5,003 features.
- Mutual information and ACO use the training data only. ACO scores candidate subsets on an inner validation split (20% of the training data).
- Logistic Regression and XGBoost are fitted on the training split and scored once on the test split.
- `numpy`, `random` and every estimator are seeded with 42; ACO uses its own `RandomState(42)`.
- DistilBERT is not part of the comparison (see the README).

## Results

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Features selected by ACO | 192 of 5,003 |
| ACO validation accuracy (inner split of the training data) | 0.8234 |
| Majority-class baseline, test accuracy | 0.5000 |
| Logistic Regression, test accuracy | 0.8400 (precision 0.85/0.83, recall 0.82/0.85, F1 0.84/0.84 for fake/real) |
| XGBoost, test accuracy | 0.8385 (precision 0.84/0.83, recall 0.83/0.84, F1 0.84/0.84 for fake/real) |
| Top features by MI | said, truth, would, total, coverup, experts, shocking, caught, hiding, behind |

Both models are about 34 points above the 50% baseline. That is expected: the labels come from a style recipe and the top features are the recipe's trigger words ("shocking", "truth", "coverup"). The two models differ by 0.15 points (6 of 4,000 articles); only one split and one seed were run, so no ranking of the two models should be drawn from it.
